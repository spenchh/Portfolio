FAULTLINE

Speculative 100GbE FPGA Engine. I am researching how physical-link errors affect speculative packet processing, and how commit/cancel architectures trade latency against data integrity.

Platform
VCU118 / Virtex UltraScale+
Target
100GbE, with single-lane validation first
Current stage
Early research and capture-feasibility study
Focus
Link errors, buffering, commit/cancel timing

The question

A packet processor can begin work before the whole packet has been validated. That saves time, but a late error can invalidate work already in flight. FAULTLINE investigates how error patterns and the point at which a decision becomes irreversible affect that tradeoff in an FPGA market-data path.

At the same bit-error rate, do measured link-error patterns produce different incorrect speculative decisions than independent random errors? How much can buffering and commit/cancel policies change the outcome, and what do they cost in latency and memory?

First milestone: useful error capture

I am starting by establishing which error events can be observed and captured with enough context for meaningful replay. This feasibility gate comes before using measured traces to make claims about speculative processing.

The intended progression is a controlled single-lane setup, followed by GTY and CMAC integration toward a 100GbE path. The work includes transceiver clocks, equalization, PCS behavior, buffering, and clock-domain and reset handling.

Experimental approach

StageEvaluation
ObserveEstablish error visibility, trace context, capture limits, and repeatability.
ReplayCompare captured error patterns with independent injection at matched bit-error rates.
Commit or cancelCompare a validated baseline with bounded speculative policies, tracking incorrect decisions and cancellation deadlines.
ImplementMeasure latency distributions, buffering requirements, FPGA resources, and timing in Vivado.

Hardware and verification

The intended architecture uses trace buffering and pending-state or journal memory to keep speculative work separate from committed state. Simulation and controlled fault injection will check ordering, backpressure, cancellation, and reset behavior before the hardware experiments.

This is research in progress. The 100GbE engine and policy comparisons are targets, and I am not reporting measured link performance or research results yet.
