Rear-Vision
Camera and Display

A low-latency rear-vision system for Northwestern's 2026 American Solar Challenge vehicle, designed and validated end to end.

Platform
Raspberry Pi, Pi Camera 3 Wide
Performance
Sub-30 ms end to end
Software
Linux, Bash, systemd
Status
Delivered, March to June 2026
View the live dashboard

The requirement

A solar vehicle has no rear window. The driver depends entirely on a camera feed, which makes latency a safety parameter rather than a comfort one. A lagging image is worse than a small one, because the driver acts on stale information at speed.

The pipeline

A Pi Camera 3 Wide feeds a Raspberry Pi over CSI, with CSI-to-HDMI extension hardware carrying the signal the length of the vehicle. The delivered pipeline runs under 30 ms end to end.

Unattended operation

Nobody logs into the vehicle to start a video feed. I wrote the startup scripting that brings the system up headless on power, applying the camera and display settings chosen during the trade study rather than whatever the device defaults to, and a systemd unit supervises it so the feed restarts itself if the pipeline dies.

That matters because the settings are the design. Resolution, framerate, and the display path were picked to hold the latency budget, so a boot that quietly fell back to defaults would give the driver a working picture with the wrong delay.

Choosing the design

The final architecture came out of a six-candidate trade study scored across latency, field of view, EMI risk, reliability, integration complexity, and cost. EMI mattered more than it might elsewhere: a solar car is a rolling high-current DC system, and a video link routed the length of the chassis sits in that environment for the whole race.
